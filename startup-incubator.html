DIRA Startup Incubator Program (DSIP)

Each year, DSIP helps Northwest businesses strengthen their foundations and pursue new opportunities. In 2027, that work brings enterprise support, practical training and a Closing Tech Summit to Bamenda.

About DSIP

DSIP is DIRA Foundation's annual programme for helping small businesses and cooperatives build the skills and systems they need to grow. The 2027 cycle will support enterprises across the Northwest Region with registration and record-keeping, while training 500 people in business, finance, marketing and technology. The year will culminate in the Closing Tech Summit, with priority given to women-led and youth-led enterprises.

The Challenge

Most enterprises in the Northwest Region operate without registration, written records or a documented financial history.

The programme responds by helping businesses and cooperatives register and keep proper records.

  • Unregistered enterprises cannot meet the requirements of banks, public buyers or investors.
  • The region loses registration fees and tax revenue from activity that goes unrecorded.
  • Young people find few openings to work in, or build, formal enterprises.
  • Many businesses are unaware of free or affordable digital tools and AI that could help them grow and work more productively.

How the Programme Works

The programme runs in three connected stages, from identifying enterprises to presenting results at the Closing Tech Summit.

Closing Tech Summit20 and 21 August 2027

Identification

Northwest Digital Readiness Survey

The survey assesses 30 to 50 enterprises, cooperative credit unions and social-economy organisations across the seven Divisions of the region.

It is financed by DIRA SAS and carried out under a signed Memorandum of Understanding with the Regional Delegation of MINPMEESA.

2

Formalisation and Training

20 July to 19 August 2027

Selected enterprises complete registration and record-keeping with a mentor, and receive coaching through the Agency for the Promotion of Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises (APME), working with MINPMEESA.

Five hundred trainees follow a common core and four expert-led tracks. Representatives of partner ministries lead sessions on opportunities, compliance and the realities of running a business or cooperative.

Bamenda Congress Hall, 20 and 21 August 2027

2027 Edition: Expected Results

1

At least 30 enterprises registered and equipped with records, each with a written starting assessment and a mentor.

2

500 people trained, with women making up at least 40 percent of participants.

3

A documented group of formalised enterprises that can be introduced, with each enterprise's consent, to financial institutions, public buyers and partners.

4

A growing ecosystem of sustainable SMEs, supported by an established, self-sustaining accelerator programme.

Governance and Reporting

Programme owner

DIRA Foundation owns the programme, is the contracting party and holds all funds in its own account.

Direction

The Executive Committee (President, Secretary, Treasurer and Logistics Planner) directs the programme, together with a Programme Director and a monthly Steering Group.

Financial controls

Payments above the General Assembly threshold need two authorising officers, and an independent chartered accountant reviews the programme accounts.

Reporting

Partners receive quarterly reports and a full programme report within 30 days of the Closing Tech Summit.
